Industrial Visit to Sahyadri Doordarshan, Worli

Sahyadri Doordarshan Broadcasting Station, Worli, Mumbai

Second Year EXTC (S.E. A & B Division)
Participants
120 Students

Devansh Sabu (Managing Director)

Faculty Guidance
Prof. Premdas Pawar & Prof. Ankit Deogharkar (11 Mar)
Prof. Keshav Chougule & Prof. Trishala Dixit (12 Mar)

Overview

The IEEE-RGIT Student Branch organized a two-day industrial visit for Second Year EXTC students to Sahyadri Doordarshan, Worli, giving them practical exposure to the real-world functioning of television broadcasting systems and communication technologies used in the media industry.

What Students Explored

01

Broadcasting Section

Engineers explained the process of television signal transmission, encoding of audio and video signals, and how signals are prepared for satellite transmission.

02

Receiver Systems & Broadcasting Tower

Students learned about parabolic antennas, satellite signal reception, and the role of the Low Noise Block Converter (LNB). Engineers also explained how satellite transponders receive, amplify, and retransmit signals.

03

Live News Studio

Students observed professional cameras, teleprompters, microphones, and studio lighting systems. Engineers explained how anchors read scripts using teleprompters and how live programs are recorded and transmitted.

04

Centralized Apparatus Room (CAR)

Students saw encoders, decoders, multiplexers, demultiplexers, and signal processing equipment, along with the role of UPS systems in uninterrupted broadcasting operations.

05

Master Control Room (MCR)

Students observed how multiple channels are monitored and how the final signal output is controlled before transmission.

Accomplishments

  • Practical exposure to real-world television broadcasting systems
  • Understanding of satellite communication, including antennas, LNB, and transponders
  • Firsthand experience observing live news studio setup and teleprompter systems
  • Knowledge of signal processing technologies such as encoding, decoding, multiplexing, and demultiplexing
  • Understanding of Centralized Apparatus Room and Master Control Room operations
  • Opportunity to interact directly with industry broadcasting engineers
  • Enhanced understanding of communication engineering concepts through hands-on observation, connecting theoretical coursework to real broadcasting infrastructure

LaTeX & Research Paper Writing Workshop

Seminar Hall, Rajiv Gandhi Institute of Technology
Participants
120 Students
Speaker
Dr. Sanjeev Chaudhari
Thakur College
Event Coordinator
Pritesh Sutar (Technical Secretary)
Entry
Free of cost — Open to all academic years

Overview

The IEEE-RGIT Student Branch organized an informative, hands-on technical session on LaTeX and research paper writing to equip students with essential academic documentation skills and introduce them to the fundamentals of writing high-quality research papers.

Session Highlights

01

LaTeX Fundamentals

Dr. Chaudhari guided students through document structure, formatting techniques, and practical usage for academic papers, demonstrating how LaTeX simplifies managing references, inserting equations, structuring sections, and preparing professional-looking documents. Students brought their own laptops for real-time hands-on practice.

02

Research Paper Structure

The session covered abstract writing, literature review, methodology presentation, result analysis, and proper referencing techniques.

03

Common Mistakes & Best Practices

Dr. Chaudhari discussed frequent errors students make in research writing and shared tips for effective technical documentation, emphasizing clarity, consistency, proper formatting, and originality.

04

Interactive Q&A

The session concluded with students asking questions on research paper submission, journal selection, plagiarism concerns, and best practices for academic writing, all addressed with practical suggestions.

Accomplishments

  • Successful Skill Development: Students learned LaTeX basics and how to structure professional research papers effectively
  • Hands-on Learning Experience: Participants practiced LaTeX live, gaining real-time experience with formatting documents, equations, and references
  • Improved Research Awareness: Students understood correct research paper structure and common mistakes to avoid
  • Expert Guidance: Valuable insights shared by Dr. Sanjeev Chaudhari on academic writing and technical documentation
  • High Student Participation: Active attendance and interaction from students across multiple academic years
  • Enhanced Confidence: Participants felt more confident preparing reports, projects, and future research publications
  • Promotion of Research Culture: Motivated students to start documenting their work using LaTeX and consider publishing research papers
  • Clarified Student Doubts: The Q&A session addressed queries on formatting, research writing practices, and documentation standards
18th March 2026

Industrial Visit — Adani Dahanu Power Station

Adani Dahanu Thermal Power Station
Organized For
Third Year EXTC (Compulsory Visit)
Participants
120 Students
Event Coordinator
Sahil Palande (Managing Director)
Event Heads
Shlok Patil (Vice Chairperson)
Harsh Rane & Ansh Khartadkar (Asst. General Secretaries)

Overview

The IEEE-RGIT Student Branch organized an industrial visit to the Adani Dahanu Thermal Power Plant Station to give Third Year EXTC students practical exposure to large-scale power generation systems, bridging the gap between theoretical learning and real-world industrial applications.

Visit Timeline

9:30 AM

Journey Begins

Students assembled at Boisar Station; the bus departed at 9:30 AM, with a breakfast break arranged around 11:30 AM before reaching the site.

12:30 PM

Introductory Session

Students were briefed on the plant overview, safety protocols, and operational guidelines. Industry experts explained thermal power generation end-to-end — coal handling, boiler operations, steam generation, turbine functioning, and electricity generation.

Advanced Tech

Advanced Technologies Observed

  • Cooling Water (CW) systems using concrete volute pumps for sea-water-based condenser cooling.
  • Digital Distributed Control, Monitoring, and Information System (DDC-MIS) for automated control of the turbine, boiler, and auxiliaries.
  • Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system for centralized transmission and distribution control.
Guided Tour

Guided Tour

Students explored boilers, turbines, generators, and cooling systems, with engineers explaining efficiency, safety measures, and large-scale operations.

Environment

Environmental Management

  • 275.3-meter-high chimney for exhaust dispersion
  • Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP) with 99.9% efficiency
  • Flue Gas Desulphurization (FGD) unit for sulphur dioxide removal
  • Four ambient air quality monitoring stations tracking PM10, PM2.5, SO₂, and NOx
3:00 PM

Lunch & Conclusion

Lunch break took place at 2:00 PM, followed by further exploration and interaction with industry professionals. The visit concluded at 3:00 PM.

Accomplishments

  • Gained practical exposure to real-world thermal power generation processes
  • Understood the complete working cycle of a thermal power plant (coal handling → steam → turbine → electricity)
  • Observed industrial-scale equipment such as boilers, turbines, generators, and cooling systems
  • Learned about advanced automation systems like DDC-MIS and SCADA used for plant control and monitoring
  • Developed awareness of modern environmental protection techniques (ESP, FGD, emission monitoring)
  • Understood the importance of safety protocols and operational discipline in industrial environments
  • Connected theoretical concepts from power systems and control systems to real-life applications
  • Interacted with industry professionals, gaining insight into current technologies and practices
  • Enhanced teamwork and peer learning through group discussions and shared observations

Introduction

TechForge'26 is an inter-collegiate technical project exhibition conceived to provide undergraduate engineering students with a credible, competitive platform to present innovative projects, demonstrate technical proficiency, and engage meaningfully with industry professionals and academic mentors.

The exhibition drew participation from Second Year (SE), Third Year (TE), and Final Year (BE) students across engineering disciplines, with projects spanning embedded systems, industrial automation, artificial intelligence, IoT, and smart technology solutions.

Sponsored By

  • Crunchy Soul
  • Mad Over Donuts
  • The Print Hive

Objectives

  • To provide a platform for technical showcasing, enabling SE, TE, and BE students to present functional prototypes and engineering solutions
  • To promote hands-on engineering and practical learning beyond theoretical concepts
  • To foster innovation and interdisciplinary thinking across embedded systems, automation, AI, robotics, and IoT
  • To enhance presentation and communication skills through live presentation to a judging panel and audience
  • To bridge the gap between academia and industry through meaningful interaction with professionals
  • To cultivate a culture of continuous improvement through judge evaluations and peer feedback

Event Planning & Preparation

Registration was circulated via WhatsApp and an official event poster advertising a prize pool of ₹15,000 / ₹10,000 / ₹5,000.

  • Registration fee: ₹400 per team
  • Team size: 2–4 members
  • Deadline: 22nd March 2026
  • Teams submitted: Project Title, Problem Statement, Project Abstract, Project Poster, Project Domain (Hardware / Software / Embedded)

Participation Policy: Open participation format with no prior screening — open to any engineering-based innovation or practical technical solution.

Departmental Coordination: All college departments were asked to nominate their five strongest student projects; internal judges from various departments supported the evaluation process.

Venue Setup: 44 display tables were arranged in the RGIT Auditorium, organized into three domain-coded sections — E (Embedded), H (Hardware), S (Software). Shared power extension boards and Wi-Fi access were provided throughout.

Volunteer Teams: Technical Team (power/setup support), Logistics Team (tables, seating, venue), Event Management Team (participant flow and announcements).

Judge Coordination: Judges were formally invited by the IEEE-RGIT Convener and Counsellor and guided through the exhibition on the day of the event.

  • Dr. Y. S. Rao appreciated the organizing committee's initiative and formally declared the exhibition open
  • Briefing of Judging Criteria by host Janhavi: Innovation and Originality, Technical Knowledge, Practical Implementation, Presentation Skills, Feasibility of the Project

Judge Interaction & Feedback

Each team presented their problem statement, methodology, working mechanism, and applications.

Judges probed deeper with technical and conceptual questions covering design methodology, working principles, programming logic/algorithms, motivation behind the project, scalability, and real-world applicability.

Judges encouraged live demonstrations of prototypes and provided constructive feedback.

Some judges suggested that promising projects could evolve into funded research initiatives or startup concepts through further refinement and industry collaboration.
